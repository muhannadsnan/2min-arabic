# 01 — Channel Concept

## One-line pitch

**2 Minute Arabic** — a daily two-minute Arabic lesson for busy people. Consistency beats intensity.

## The problem we solve

Most people who "want to learn a language" fail the same way: not from lack of talent or material,
but from **inconsistency**. They go all-in for a few weeks, life gets busy, they stop, they feel guilty,
and they restart from zero months later.

Long lessons make this worse. A 30-minute video is a decision you have to make every day.
A 2-minute video is not a decision — it's a habit you can keep on the worst day of your week.

## The founder story (the heart of the channel)

This is the story told in Day 1, and referenced again whenever the channel needs to re-motivate viewers.

- Arabic is my **mother tongue** — that's why I teach it.
- Spanish is my **fourth language**. I've been learning it for **two years**.
- I learned it in bursts: one month I'd go all in — notes, lots of videos, many many conversations
  to listen to — and get good at it. Then I'd get too busy for **two months** and touch nothing.
- After two years, I can finally hold a **simple conversation with a native speaker** — at **A1–A2** level.
- The lesson: if I had found **just two minutes every single day** for those two years,
  I'd be far better today.
- So I'm building the thing I wish I had: two minutes a day, every day.

Why this story works on YouTube: it's honest, it's relatable (everyone has a half-learned language),
and it makes the host a **fellow learner who made the mistake**, not a perfect teacher lecturing.
Being a native Arabic speaker gives authority on the *language*; being a struggling Spanish learner
gives authority on the *learning*.

## The 1% principle (the channel's philosophy)

| | Every day for a year | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Get 1% better | 1.01 ^ 365 | **≈ 37.8×** better |
| Get 1% worse | 0.99 ^ 365 | **≈ 0.03** — almost nothing left |

Small things done daily compound. Big efforts done randomly don't.
Two minutes is the "1%" of your day. Use this math in Day 1, and bring it back at milestones
(Day 7, Day 30, Day 100…).

## The promise

> "Don't make my mistake. Give me two minutes a day, and in 30 days you'll start your first
> conversation in Arabic. No fluff. No excuses."

The promise is deliberately **concrete and checkable**: a *first* conversation — greeting, names,
how are you, where are you from, simple wants, goodbye — not fluency.
The [30-day curriculum](04-curriculum-30-days.md) is built backwards from exactly that goal,
so the promise is honest.

> The original wording was "no bullshit". The scripts use "No fluff. No excuses." to stay
> family-friendly for YouTube's recommendation system; swap it back if you prefer the edgier tone.

## Audience

- **Primary:** busy adults (18–45) who want Arabic for travel, work, heritage, faith, a partner's family,
  or curiosity — and who have failed before because they "didn't have time".
- **Secondary:** heritage learners (children of Arab families) who understand some but can't speak.
- **Level:** absolute beginner → A1/A2.

## What makes it different

1. **Hard 2-minute cap.** The format *is* the message.
2. **A day counter.** "Day 14" creates a streak viewers don't want to break.
3. **Speak-out-loud pauses.** Every video makes the viewer *say* Arabic, not just watch it.
4. **Honest host.** A native speaker who openly shares his own language-learning failure.
5. **Recurring story world.** Sami and Lina reappear in conversations and stories, so vocabulary
   is recycled naturally.

## Which Arabic?

The channel teaches **simple Modern Standard Arabic (MSA / فُصْحَى)** pronounced the way it is spoken
in real conversation (no case endings at the end of phrases):

- It is understood in **every** Arab country; a dialect would lock viewers into one region.
- AI voices read MSA well; they read dialects poorly.
- Very common everyday expressions (e.g. *al-hamdu lillaah*, *ma'a s-salaama*) are identical in MSA
  and the dialects, so viewers still sound natural.

Later, a recurring "**Street version**" segment can show how a phrase sounds in Levantine / Egyptian /
Gulf dialects — great for comments and engagement.

## Tone of voice

- Warm, direct, a little cheeky. Short sentences. No academic words.
- Talk to **one person** ("you"), never "you guys".
- Celebrate the streak, not perfection.
- Never make the viewer feel behind. Missed a day? "Just do today's two minutes."
