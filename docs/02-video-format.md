# 02 — Video Format

Every video follows the same skeleton. Viewers learn the rhythm, and production becomes a routine.

## Target length

**~2:00** (acceptable range 1:45–2:15). Hard ceiling **2:59** so every video also qualifies as a YouTube Short
(Shorts can be up to 3 minutes).

Rule of thumb for English narration: ~150 words ≈ 1 minute. Arabic lines + pauses take roughly
the same time as their English share, so a script with **~220–260 English words** lands near 2:00.

## The skeleton

| # | Segment | Time | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** | 0:00–0:05 | One line that makes a scroller stop. Unique per video. |
| 2 | **Channel intro** | ~12 s | The fixed welcome + promise (below). Same every video. |
| 3 | **Today** | ~8 s | "Today is Day N. Today you'll…" — what they'll be able to do after 2 minutes. |
| 4 | **Lesson** | ~75–85 s | The content, in one of the 5 formats below. |
| 5 | **Challenge / recap** | ~10 s | One thing to say out loud or do today. |
| 6 | **Outro** | ~10 s | Fixed sign-off + tomorrow teaser + مَعَ السَّلَامَة. |

> **Why the hook comes before the intro:** in the first 3 seconds viewers decide whether to stay.
> A fixed intro first would lose them. Hook → intro keeps both the retention and the ritual.

## Standard channel intro (every video)

```say:narrator
Welcome to 2 Minute Arabic — one tiny lesson, every day. Arabic is my mother tongue, and my promise is simple: two minutes a day, and in thirty days you'll have your first conversation in Arabic.
```

Record/generate this **once** and reuse the same clip in every video (it also becomes a recognizable sound).
Optionally make 2–3 variants later so long-time viewers don't tire of it.

## Standard outro (every video)

```say:narrator
That's your two minutes for today. Come back tomorrow — same place, two minutes. Subscribe so you don't break your streak.
```
```say:teacher
مَعَ السَّلَامَة!
```

Just before the standard outro, each script adds one short, video-specific line:
"Day N done — tomorrow: …".

## Content rules (owner, 2026-09-27)

- **Easy, but not too easy.** Every video teaches **at least 5–8 new words/phrases** on top of recycled ones — the promise
  is a real first conversation after 30 days, so each day must move the viewer forward.
- **Days 1–5:** every Arabic line is followed by its English meaning (spoken).
- **From Day 6:** **no spoken English translation after each Arabic line** — the meaning is on the card
  (Arabic · transliteration · English). Spoken English is for the hook, intro, short cues ("Listen.", "Your turn."),
  new-word explanations and the outro. This keeps videos within ~2 minutes while teaching more.
- All Arabic is spoken by the owner (recorded); see [10-production-flow.md](10-production-flow.md).

## The 5 lesson formats

The curriculum rotates through five formats. Each has a fixed internal pattern.

### 1. Phrases (e.g. Day 2)
For each phrase: **English meaning (narrator) → Arabic (teacher) → ⏸️ pause 2 s** (viewer repeats).
6–10 phrases per video. On screen: Arabic with tashkeel · transliteration · English.

### 2. Conversation (e.g. Day 3)
1. **Listen** — the full dialogue once, no translation, images only (skip if it pushes the video past ~2:20).
2. **Understand** — line by line: Arabic line → English meaning.
3. **Role-play** — only one character speaks; ⏸️ pauses where the viewer answers as the other character.

Max ~10 lines. Reuse at least 70% known words; introduce at most 2–3 new ones.

### 3. Story (e.g. Day 4)
1. **Story with meaning** — each sentence: Arabic → English, one image per sentence.
2. **Arabic only** — the whole story again, no English. Viewer checks how much they understand.
3. **Key words** — 3 words to remember.

6–8 very short sentences. A tiny plot twist makes it memorable.

### 4. Grammar (e.g. Day 5)
1. **One rule**, stated in one sentence of plain English (no grammar jargon, or explain it instantly).
2. **3–5 examples**, each: Arabic → literal word-for-word → natural English.
3. **Your turn** — narrator gives English, ⏸️ 3 s pause, teacher gives the answer.

Only one rule per video. Always built from words the viewer already knows.

### 5. Review (every 7th day)
A quiz: English prompt → ⏸️ 3 s → Arabic answer. Cover the whole week. Ends with a milestone
message (e.g. "7 days — 1.01⁷ — you're already 7% better than a week ago").

## Teaching rules (all formats)

- **Make them speak.** At least 3 pauses per video where the viewer says Arabic out loud.
- **Recycle.** Every new video reuses words from the previous 2–3 videos.
- **Arabic always in the Arabic voice.** The English narrator never pronounces Arabic words —
  the teacher voice does. (The narrator may say a name like "Sami".)
- **Meaning first or right after** — never leave an Arabic line unexplained the first time it appears.
- **Show everything.** Every Arabic line appears on screen: Arabic script with tashkeel, transliteration, English.
