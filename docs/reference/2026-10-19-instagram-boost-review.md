## Review by section

### Billing: the biggest easy win

Since February 2024, boosts bought in the Instagram iOS app are billed through Apple, which keeps a 30% service charge on the payment ([Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/ipad-app/898715828241123)). The "Apple service fee: To be calculated" line on your budget screen is that charge. Meta's own workarounds: boost from instagram.com in a mobile or desktop browser, use Meta Ads Manager or Business Suite, or add prepaid funds via the web and then boost in the app ([Meta announcement](https://projecter.workplace.com/business/news/new-ways-for-small-business-to-boost-and-avoid-apple-service-charges)). Same targeting, same delivery, no fee.

### Goal

"Optimized mix" lets Meta spend on likes, comments and messages, which don't grow the account. Your goal is followers, so choose "Visit your profile" (labelled best for follows). If you keep the mix, tap Edit under it and set the preferred goal to follows.

### Locations

The list is mostly large Muslim-majority, non-Arab countries, which is a sensible market for Arabic learners. Three problems:

- **China:** Instagram is blocked there. Remove it.
- **Price gap:** India, Pakistan, Nigeria, Indonesia and the Philippines cost a fraction of France and Germany. In one ad set, delivery chases the cheapest impressions, so the EU countries will get almost nothing.
- **Language fit:** viewers must understand the language your on-screen explanation and caption use. If it's English, Turkey, Uzbekistan, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan and Afghanistan will convert poorly.

### Interests

Advantage+ is on, so interests are only suggestions: Meta starts there, then widens. That makes the starting point matter. Training, Education and Learning are generic (fitness, schools, courses) and inflate the audience to \~300M. Replace them with Arabic language, Learning Arabic, Arabic calligraphy and Middle East culture, plus Quran and Islamic studies if your lessons serve that learner. Keep Language education, Foreign language, Language school and Duolingo.

### Budget and impressions

$10/day for 5 days is a sound first test. But the forecast of up to 1.38M impressions for $50 works out to about $0.04–0.07 per 1,000 views. Reach that cheap usually means fast scrolling, low-attention placements; it won't translate into loyal followers. Judge the result by cost per follower, not reach.

### Advertiser and payer

Both set to "2 Minute Arabic" is fine. Because France and Germany are EU countries, this is required and will be public in the Ad Library for a year, but not shown on the ad itself.

### Creative (not in the screenshots)

The post you boost matters more than any setting. Pick a reel that already has your best organic watch time, with the lesson's hook in the first 2 seconds and "Follow for a new Arabic lesson every day" on screen or in the caption.

## Countries to target

Run two separate boosts: tier 1 for cheap, high-intent growth, and tier 2 for followers worth more later. Mixing them in one boost sends nearly all spend to tier 1.

| Tier | Countries | Why they fit | Cost per follower |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Growth | Pakistan, Indonesia, Malaysia, Bangladesh, Nigeria, India | Huge Muslim populations learning Arabic to read the Quran; good English in most | Very low |
| 1. Growth | Philippines | Many work or plan to work in the Gulf and need everyday Arabic; strong English | Very low |
| 2. Value | UK, US, Canada, Australia | English-speaking; Muslim diaspora, heritage learners and general language learners | High |
| 2. Value | France, Germany, Netherlands, Belgium, Sweden | Large Arab and Muslim diaspora; second-generation kids who speak some Arabic but can't read it | High |
| 2. Value | Spain | Al-Andalus heritage, a large Moroccan community and real interest in Arabic; your Spanish lets you add Spanish captions | Medium–high |
| 2. Value | Norway | Your home market; you can answer comments in Norwegian | High |
| 3. Small test | UAE, Qatar | Most residents are expats who need daily Arabic; but locals already speak it, so keep the test small | Medium |
| Drop | China | Instagram is blocked | — |
| Drop unless captions match | Turkey, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Afghanistan | Weak English; add only with captions in their language | Low |

EU countries (France, Germany, Netherlands, Belgium, Sweden, Spain) trigger the advertiser/payer disclosure, which you have already filled in.

### Cheap vs. high-value countries

Tier 1 isn't bad, it just pays back differently. Followers there are cheap and motivated, but they earn little YouTube ad revenue per view and have less money for paid courses later. Tier 2 followers cost several times more but are worth more if you monetize.

One side effect: if most followers come from one tier, Instagram keeps showing your posts to similar people. A follower base that is 90% tier 1 will pull your organic reach that way too.

Suggested split for the first $50: about $35 on tier 1 and $15 on tier 2, to learn each tier's real cost per follower.

### Fit with the product plan

The plan is low-priced monthly e-notebooks for beginners, sold in volume. That model suits tier 1, so tier 1 stays the main growth market rather than just a follower source. Two conditions decide whether it pays:

- **Local payment methods:** many buyers in Pakistan, Nigeria, India, Indonesia and the Philippines don't use international cards. Check that your store accepts what they use (e.g. UPI in India, GCash in the Philippines, e-wallets in Indonesia) before spending on those countries.
- **Regional pricing:** one global price is either too high for tier 1 or too low for tier 2. Price by country, e.g. a lower price in tier 1 and a higher one in tier 2 for the same notebook.

Tier 2 is still worth keeping: fewer buyers, but each pays more and pays easily by card. Once the store is live, compare cost per sale by tier, not cost per follower, and move budget to whichever tier sells cheapest.

## Sales strategy

The plan is sound in direction, but it has too many small products and a lifetime offer that is priced and pitched too early. Simplify to one membership plus two books, and hold the lifetime pass until there is an archive worth buying.

### Proposed product line (Master's draft)

| Product | What it is | Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly note | PDF, a couple of pages, best of the week's YouTube lessons | Weekly |
| Monthly note | Longer PDF, highlights of the month | Monthly |
| Monthly bonus | Material not yet covered on the channel | Monthly |
| Grammar book | Arabic grammar | Seasonal |
| Story reader | Arabic story reading | Seasonal |
| Lifetime pass | "Your Arabic teacher forever": full archive, all levels, all future content | One-off, $499 |

Pricing idea: three country stages, from $2.99–9.99 in the lowest stage, rising about 33% per stage.

### Review of the product line

- **Weekly and monthly notes overlap.** Both repackage free YouTube content; the monthly one recaps the weekly ones. Few people pay separately for a recap of what they can watch free.
- **The monthly bonus is the real value.** New material is what people pay for; make it the core.
- **Too many separate purchases.** Five products × three price stages = 15 prices, and a buyer paying weekly faces 4–5 checkouts a month.
- **The books are strong.** Grammar and story reading are evergreen, easy to explain, and sell for years.

Recommended line-up:

| Product | Contents | Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Membership | Weekly notes + monthly summary + monthly bonus material, all in one | Monthly subscription |
| Grammar book | Seasonal grammar book | One-off purchase |
| Story reader | Seasonal story reading book | One-off purchase |
| Lifetime pass (later) | Everything above, past and future | One-off, launched once the archive exists |

### Country pricing

A 33% step per stage is too flat: the top stage would only be about 1.8× the bottom, while spending power differs far more. Set the low stage at roughly 40% of the high price and the mid stage at about 70%.

| Product | High stage | Mid stage | Low stage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Membership (per month) | $7.99 | $5.49 | $2.99 |
| Grammar book | $15.99 | $10.99 | $5.99 |
| Story reader | $12.99 | $8.99 | $4.99 |
| Lifetime pass (founding price) | $249 | $179 | $99 |

- **High stage:** US, UK, Canada, Australia, Western Europe, Nordics, Gulf states
- **Mid stage:** Malaysia, Turkey, Eastern Europe, Latin America, North Africa
- **Low stage:** India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nigeria, Indonesia, Philippines, Egypt

Avoid single products below about $2.99: per-sale payment fees are partly fixed, so they take a large share of very small sales. Sell through a merchant-of-record store (e.g. Lemon Squeezy or Paddle) so EU/UK/Norwegian VAT and per-country pricing are handled for you.

### The lifetime pass idea

A top-price offer is a good idea: it anchors the membership as cheap and brings cash upfront. The version as drafted has five problems:

1. **No archive yet.** The channel started in October 2026; a $499 promise of "everything" has little to show today.
2. **Price.** $499 equals about 5 years of a $7.99 membership. Lifetime deals usually land at 2–3 years' worth. In the low stage, $499 is out of reach for almost everyone.
3. **"Your teacher forever" is a promise you can't scale.** It implies personal teaching. With hundreds of buyers that becomes unworkable, and unmet promises lead to refund requests and chargebacks. Promise content access, not personal teaching.
4. **Define "forever".** Write it as "all content published by 2 Minute Arabic, for as long as it is published", so the terms are clear if the project ever ends.
5. **Pitching it in every video hurts the videos.** In a 2-minute lesson, a sales pitch costs watch time, which drives reach. Mention it in one line at the end, the bio, a pinned comment and dedicated promo posts.

Recommended version: launch it after about 6–12 months as a **Founding Lifetime Pass**, capped at the first 100 buyers, at the founding prices above. Raise the price as the archive grows; the cap creates urgency and limits long-term obligations.

### Rollout order

1. Grow followers with the boosts (sections above)
2. Launch the membership and collect the first paying members
3. Release the first grammar book
4. Launch the Founding Lifetime Pass once the archive holds several months of material

## What to measure after the boost

Judge the boost by cost per follower and whether those followers stick, not by reach.

- [ ] **Cost per follower:** $ spent ÷ follows from the ad (in the boost's Insights)
- [ ] **Profile visit → follow rate:** if visits are high but follows low, fix the bio and pinned posts
- [ ] **Watch time on the boosted reel:** low average watch time means the hook, not the targeting, is the problem
- [ ] **Follower countries a week later:** check that new followers match the countries you paid for
- [ ] **Unfollows after 7 days:** a big drop means cheap, low-intent reach
- [ ] **YouTube spillover:** clicks on the bio link to @2MinArabic

Open questions: which reel are you boosting, and what language are its on-screen explanation and caption in?
